DimmerKV: KV cache compression for LLM inference

Dim the KV cache instead of deleting it. DimmerKV stores a language model's KV cache at 2–5% of its FP16 size and still answers questions about the compressed text as well as the uncompressed cache.

The problem with KV cache eviction

A 16K-token conversation on Llama-3.2-3B needs 1.75 GB of KV cache. Eviction methods such as H2O and SnapKV keep the tokens that received the most attention and delete the rest. That fails when the question comes after compression: before the question exists, the facts it will need were never heavily attended. In our tests they sat at the 30–35th percentile of H2O's scores, so H2O deleted them.

How it works

  1. Transform once per model. Undo RoPE on the keys, join all layers and heads into one vector per token, and fit one PCA basis (kvtc-style transform coding).
  2. Give every token its own precision. One byte budget is spent by error removed per byte, from dropped to fine bit-planes, with a floor at kvtc's precision and extra bytes for tokens the question attends to.
  3. Store progressive bit-planes in tiers. A hot tier holds the first planes; a cold tier holds the rest. Demote a conversation by dropping planes, with no re-encoding.
  4. Refine when the question arrives. Score the coarse cache with the question and fetch full precision for its top ~1% of tokens.
  5. Answer from the codes. Keys are rebuilt one layer at a time; values stay in coefficient space. The full FP16 cache never exists while answering.
